# ATO Maintenance Schedule for Nano Tanks

## Intent
Maintenance / prevention article for nano reef and open-top tank owners who want a realistic inspection rhythm that reduces false alarms, overfills, sensor drift, and slow reliability decay.

## Working title
ATO Maintenance Schedule for Nano Tanks: A Realistic Routine That Helps You Catch Problems Before They Turn Dumb

## Search intent
- Primary intent: figure out how often an ATO should be checked, cleaned, and sanity-tested in a small tank
- Secondary intent: prevent failures without inventing obsessive maintenance theater
- User state: wants a practical routine, not vague advice like “inspect regularly”

## Angle
A realistic maintenance guide built around failure prevention, not fake precision. The point is to help small-tank owners notice drift, fouling, and layout problems early enough to keep the system trustworthy.

## Outline

### Intro
- ATO systems usually do not fail out of nowhere. They drift into failure through dirt, salt creep, mount movement, neglected tubing, and ignored warning behavior.
- In nano tanks, that drift matters faster because the water volume is less forgiving.
- The goal is not over-maintaining everything. The goal is catching the obvious problems before they stack into something worse.

### What maintenance is actually trying to prevent
- sensor fouling
- float sticking or drag
- tubing route changes and siphon risk
- bracket drift
- reservoir contamination or sloppy refill habits
- nuisance alarms turning into alert fatigue

### Suggested maintenance rhythm
#### Every top-off or refill
- quick visual check of sensor position
- quick check of tubing route and outlet placement
- look for obvious salt creep, residue, or weird waterline behavior

#### Weekly
- wipe down obvious salt creep around the sensing area
- confirm the normal operating waterline still matches expectation
- note whether the system is topping off in a way that feels normal

#### Every 2-4 weeks
- clean the sensing surface or inspect float movement more intentionally
- inspect brackets, clips, and cable routing
- check for recurring bubbles, splash, or turbulence near the sensor
- inspect reservoir cleanliness and refill habits

#### Periodically after any change
- retest behavior after moving tubing, cleaning mounts, changing pumps, changing return flow, or swapping reservoir position
- re-evaluate whether the setup is still conservative enough for the tank

### Why exact intervals are hard to promise
- salt creep differs by tank
- splash and turbulence differ by layout
- maintenance burden differs by sensor style
- owner tolerance and observation habits differ too

So the right answer is not one magic schedule. It is a baseline rhythm with more attention when conditions get dirtier, noisier, or less stable.

### Warning signs that maintenance is overdue
- false alarms becoming more frequent
- top-off cycles that feel longer or weirder than usual
- visible residue on the sensing area
- waterline drifting from normal behavior
- mounts that no longer feel secure
- the owner starting to ignore alerts because they happen “all the time”

### Maintenance mistakes to avoid
- cleaning the sensor but not checking alignment afterward
- assuming a backup sensor removes the need for inspection
- letting reservoir convenience drive risky sizing decisions
- ignoring tubing route because the sensor seems to be the only thing that matters
- inventing fake certainty about maintenance intervals without respecting tank conditions
