- In nano reef tanks, the wrong ATO is not just inconvenient; it can create rapid salinity drift or an outsized mess.

- For small tanks, graceful failure behavior, maintenance reality, and conservative setup matter more than gadget appeal.

### How to evaluate this category
#### Reliability / failure modes
- Does the system have meaningful backup logic?
- How vulnerable is it to fouling, sticking, or siphon mistakes?

#### Maintenance burden
- How often does the sensing system need cleaning or inspection?
- How easy is it to notice when behavior starts drifting?

#### Reservoir / pump considerations
- Is the pump/controller pairing appropriate for a small system?
- Does the setup make it too easy to create a siphon or overdose event?

#### Noise / clutter
- Does the system fit a bedroom, office, or display tank environment?

#### Safety / fail-safes
- What prevents one small sensor error from becoming a much larger problem?

### Common mistakes
- Buying for features instead of failure behavior
- Using too large a reservoir without understanding downside
- Ignoring tubing route and siphon risk
- Assuming redundancy works without periodic testing and cleaning

### Final recommendation framing
- The right recommendation should depend on user tolerance for maintenance, failure risk, and footprint—not price alone.
- A simpler, better-understood system may be safer than a feature-rich one the owner will not maintain properly.
