# Why Your ATO Keeps Overfilling a Nano Tank

## Intent
Troubleshooting / problem-aware / high-anxiety article for nano reef and open-top tank owners dealing with repeated overfill events or suspicious top-off behavior.

## Working title
Why Your ATO Keeps Overfilling a Nano Tank: The Most Likely Causes, Fixes, and Safety Checks

## Search intent
- Primary intent: diagnose overfilling behavior quickly and safely
- Secondary intent: determine whether cleaning, rerouting, or replacing a component is the right move
- User state: worried about flooding, salinity swings, or hidden failure chains

## Angle
Calm, ranked diagnosis guide that explains the most likely causes first, tells the reader what to check immediately, and avoids pretending every overfill means the whole system is junk.

## Outline

### Intro
- ATO overfilling in a nano tank is not just annoying; small water volumes make mistakes matter faster.
- The main risks are salinity swings, reservoir overdosing, floor/wiring messes, and false confidence after a temporary fix.
- Most overfills come from a short list of repeat causes, and the fastest path is to check them in probability order.

### Symptom summary
- Reservoir drains faster than expected
- Water level rises above normal top-off line
- Pump keeps running longer than it should
- Alarm or controller behaves inconsistently
- Tank salinity drifts after the event

### Fast triage checklist
- Unplug or disable the top-off system before repeated testing
- Check whether the sensor is fouled, blocked, bubbled, or misaligned
- Inspect tubing route for siphon behavior
- Confirm the reservoir size is not large enough to create a bigger problem than the tank can tolerate
- Check whether the pump stopped when it should have, or whether the controller misread the water line

### Likely causes ranked by probability
1. Optical sensor fouling or false reading
2. Float switch sticking or hanging up
3. Tubing layout creating a siphon after the pump stops
4. Sensor placement drift after cleaning, bumping, or salt creep buildup
5. Controller, timer, or backup shutoff not behaving as expected
6. Reservoir setup turning a small failure into a larger flood/salinity problem

### When to stop troubleshooting and replace a component
- Repeated false triggers after cleaning and remounting
- Mechanical switches that no longer move consistently
- Controllers with inconsistent behavior you cannot isolate confidently
- Any setup where the user no longer trusts the fail-safe chain

### Safety / risk-control notes
- Small tanks swing fast; treat overfill behavior as a system risk, not just an annoyance
- Avoid assuming a backup sensor makes any layout safe
- Keep reservoir sizing conservative until the system has proven reliable over time
