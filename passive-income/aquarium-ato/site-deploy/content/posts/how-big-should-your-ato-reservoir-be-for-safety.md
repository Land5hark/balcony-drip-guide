## The useful question
The useful question is not just how long you want to go between refills. The useful question is how much downside you are willing to store next to a small tank if the ATO misreads the water level, a siphon develops, or a controller behaves badly.

That is why reservoir size is part of the safety system, not just a convenience decision.

## Why reservoir size matters more in nano tanks
In a nano setup:
- there is less water volume to absorb mistakes
- a bad top-off event can move salinity faster
- the physical mess can feel absurdly large relative to the tank
- convenience can quietly overpower caution if the setup has not proven itself yet

## What should guide sizing
### 1. Tank water volume
What looks like a modest reservoir on a larger system can represent a much bigger downside on a nano tank.

### 2. Trust in the setup
A new or recently changed ATO deserves a more conservative reservoir choice than a setup that has behaved predictably over time.

### 3. Time away from the tank
Longer absences push people toward larger reservoirs. That tradeoff may be reasonable, but it should be a conscious tradeoff, not an accidental one.

### 4. Failure consequences in the real room
Think beyond the tank:
- what happens to the floor?
- what happens near power strips, stands, or cabinetry?
- how annoying is the recovery if the system fails while nobody is watching?

## A conservative way to think about it
A sane default approach is:
- start smaller when the setup is new or not fully trusted
- increase convenience only after the behavior proves predictable
- think in terms of worst-case outcome, not just refill annoyance
- remember that reservoir sizing and tubing layout work together as part of the risk profile

## Bad assumptions to avoid
- “my backup sensor makes reservoir size irrelevant”
- “I only need to worry about the sensor, not the volume available to dose”
- “larger always means better because I refill less often”
- “small tank means small risk by default”

## When a smaller reservoir is the better choice
A smaller reservoir is often smarter when:
- the setup is still being dialed in
- the sensor area gets dirty quickly
- tubing route or mount stability still feels provisional
- the owner cares more about downside control than refill convenience
