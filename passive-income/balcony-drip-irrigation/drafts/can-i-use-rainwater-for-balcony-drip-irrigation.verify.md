# Verification Notes: Rainwater for Balcony Drip Irrigation

## Verification Status: VERIFIED

## Claims Verified

### 1. Rainwater Collection Math ✅
**Claim**: 1-inch rainfall on 10 sq ft yields ~6 gallons
**Formula**: 10 sq ft × 1/12 ft (1 inch) × 7.48 gallons/cubic foot = 6.23 gallons
**Status**: ✅ **VERIFIED** - Math is correct
**Efficiency note**: 70-80% collection efficiency is a reasonable conservative estimate accounting for first-flush diversion, spillage, and imperfect collection surface angles. Actual efficiency varies by setup quality.

### 2. Water Requirements ✅
**Claim**: Large tomato pot needs 1-2 gallons every 2-3 days in summer
**Status**: ✅ **VERIFIED** - Reasonable estimate based on:
- Container evapotranspiration rates for 5-gallon containers
- Summer heat and wind exposure typical of balconies
- Tomato plant water demands during fruiting stage

### 3. Filtration Specifications ✅
**Claim**: 100-200 mesh screen filter minimum for drip systems
**Status**: ✅ **VERIFIED** - Industry standard for drip irrigation. 
- 100 mesh = 150 micron
- 200 mesh = 74 micron
- Standard emitters require ~150 micron filtration (100 mesh)
- Fine emitters benefit from 74 micron (200 mesh)

### 4. pH Levels ✅
**Claim**: Rainwater pH 5.0-6.0, concrete raises pH
**Status**: ✅ **VERIFIED**
- Rainwater pH: 5.0-6.0 (naturally acidic from atmospheric CO2 dissolution)
- Urban rainwater: may be lower due to pollution (4.5-5.5)
- Concrete leaches calcium carbonate (alkaline), raising pH
- Most plants prefer pH 6.0-7.0; rainwater is generally beneficial

## Safety Notes (Verified)

- ✅ **Mosquito breeding**: Confirmed risk - standing water breeds mosquitoes in 7-10 days
- ✅ **Weight limits**: 15-gallon reservoir = ~125 lbs (water 8.34 lbs/gal + container)
- ✅ **Electrical safety**: GFCI protection required for pumps near water (NEC code)

## Legal/HOA Considerations
