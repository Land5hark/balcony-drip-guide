# Affiliate Program Scorecard for Niche Selection

Use this before committing to a niche, and again before making a merchant the primary monetization path.

## Rule zero
A niche does **not** pass monetization validation unless at least one affiliate path is real and worth the effort.

That means:
1. the program exists
2. the niche/merchant fit is credible
3. the economics are not insultingly weak
4. payout/approval friction is acceptable
5. trust damage from using the merchant is low

## Required metrics
Score every serious merchant/program candidate on these:
- commission %
- cookie/referral window
- average order value
- conversion rate
- approval difficulty
- payout threshold
- reversal rate
- brand fit / trust

## Two-stage evaluation

### Stage 1 — hard gate
Before deeper work, confirm:
- real public affiliate path exists **or** direct evidence inside a network/dashboard exists
- merchant actually sells products/services central to the niche
- commission is not obviously dead-on-arrival for the niche economics
- no obvious trust problem, scam signal, or policy red flag

If this stage fails, the niche is not ready.

### Stage 2 — weighted score
Score each category 1-5.

#### 1. Commission % — weight 20
- 5 = excellent economics for niche
- 4 = good
- 3 = acceptable
- 2 = thin
- 1 = garbage / not worth effort

#### 2. Cookie / referral window — weight 10
- 5 = long window, generous attribution
- 3 = normal/acceptable
- 1 = very short / fragile attribution

#### 3. Average order value — weight 15
- 5 = high ticket / strong basket value
- 3 = moderate
- 1 = low-ticket, hard to make meaningful revenue

#### 4. Conversion rate potential — weight 15
Estimate from brand recognition, product-market fit, price competitiveness, checkout friction, and purchase intent.
- 5 = likely strong converter
- 3 = decent but uncertain
- 1 = weak converter

#### 5. Approval difficulty — weight 10
- 5 = easy / low friction
- 3 = moderate
- 1 = hard, selective, or unclear

#### 6. Payout threshold — weight 5
- 5 = low threshold / easy to get paid
- 3 = tolerable
- 1 = annoying threshold or payout structure

#### 7. Reversal rate / lock risk — weight 10
- 5 = low reversal risk / sane lock structure
- 3 = normal
- 1 = ugly clawback/reversal behavior

#### 8. Brand fit / trust — weight 15
- 5 = obvious fit, strong trust signal, no weirdness
- 3 = workable but not ideal
- 1 = trust-eroding or off-brand

## Scoring formula
Weighted score out of 100:

`score = (commission*20 + cookie*10 + aov*15 + conversion*15 + approval*10 + threshold*5 + reversal*10 + trust*15) / 5`

## Decision bands
- **85-100** = excellent primary merchant candidate
- **70-84** = viable primary or strong secondary
- **55-69** = usable backup only
- **below 55** = weak; do not anchor niche economics to this

## Niche-level decision rule
A niche is monetization-ready only if it has:
- **1 primary merchant candidate scoring 70+**, and
- **at least 1 backup path** scoring 55+ or a fallback like Amazon that fills product gaps

If no merchant hits 70+, either:
- change the monetization model
- change the niche angle
- or reject the niche

## Fast operating checklist
For each new niche:
1. identify 3-5 likely merchants/programs
2. verify whether each program actually exists
3. fill in the scorecard
4. rank primary / secondary / fallback
5. reject niches with no credible merchant stack
6. only then start building the site/content pipeline

## Notes for physical-product niches
Physical-product affiliate niches often tolerate lower commission **only if**:
- average order value is strong
- conversion is strong
- trust fit is excellent
- the content naturally supports repeated buyer-intent traffic

## Notes for Sharky/Spike operating style
Do not let a niche pass just because it is interesting or easy to write about.
The niche must have a believable path to tracked transactions and actual payout.
