## The fastest path to a working system

If you want the least painful path, start with your water source instead of the kit name. Most bad balcony watering setups fail because the buyer chose a product category before checking the boring constraints: faucet access, pressure, where excess water drains, and whether tubing can stay in place without annoying the landlord or the neighbor below.

### If you have a faucet

Use a hose timer, pressure reducer, filter, mainline tubing, and short 1/4-inch runs to each container. This is the most reliable setup for 6 or more pots because the water source is effectively unlimited and the timer handles consistency.

### If you do not have a faucet

Do not start with a normal hose-end kit. It will usually assume pressure and flow you do not have. Start with a reservoir-fed plan instead: gravity bucket for simple small setups, solar pump for automation, or a compact apartment kit if you need something cleaner-looking.

### If you are trying to keep plants alive while traveling

Build around reservoir capacity and failure checks, not just timer features. A timer is useful only if the water source can cover the full trip and the emitters are not clogged, kinked, or dumping water into one pot while starving the rest.

## What not to buy first

Avoid buying a smart timer as the first part if you have not solved pressure, filtering, tubing layout, and emitter flow. Smart scheduling cannot fix a clogged emitter or a balcony with no usable faucet.

Avoid buying a large all-in-one kit before counting containers. Many kits include parts that make sense for garden beds but are awkward on balconies: too much 1/2-inch tubing, too few pot-friendly emitters, or fittings meant for ground-level layouts.

Avoid relying on terracotta spikes or tiny bottle drippers for trips longer than a weekend. They can help one or two low-demand pots, but they are not a full balcony watering system.
